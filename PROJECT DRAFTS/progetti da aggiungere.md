SOFABLE

<h2>Separè</h2>

The Separè is a fourth element in the Metis system — formally coherent but structurally independent. Unlike the three seating configurations, which share the same base and cushion components, the room divider required an entirely new module developed from scratch.

The module follows the same formal philosophy: its geometry is derived from the Sofable logo, its curves share the language of the base frame. But its construction logic is different — a standalone unit, interlockable with rotations of 90° from each adjacent module, generating a continuous pattern inspired by chainmail. The result is a sculptural structure that extends vertically across the full height of a wall with rhythm and fluidity.

<h2>The brief</h2>

The starting point was the concept of Orografie: reinterpreting the fundamental amphibious concept in furniture design, with a reference to Mediterranean culture. The brief asked to move beyond traditional typological categorisations, to design new functions before new forms.

The theoretical reference was twofold: on one side the morphological and typological analysis of Salvatore Greco, on the other the need to recover the critical and radical capacity of Italian design as described by Orografie's Art Director:x Vincenzo Castellana.

Five principles guided the process, condensed here rather than laid out as a checklist:

<p style="font-family:var(--mono);font-size:.72rem;line-height:1.9;color:var(--ink-m);margin:20px 0;"><strong style="color:var(--teal);">Functions.</strong> Start from functions rather than forms to arrive at a new approach to needs. <strong style="color:var(--teal);">Observe.</strong> Pay attention to the improper use people make of things, to understand a need that can become a design seed. <strong style="color:var(--teal);">Care.</strong> Dedicate care and attention to the objects around us, because in antiquity this meant respect for the context and for those who act within it. <strong style="color:var(--teal);">Preserve.</strong> Design objects meant to be preserved and maintained rather than quickly replaced, planning the lifespan of things so they age healthily over time. <strong style="color:var(--teal);">Quality.</strong> The quality of design forms, construction details, and raw materials is the best guarantee of permanence we have.</p>
