<div class="proj-lead" markdown="1">
Urban proximity logistics fails in three predictable places: vehicles too large for city streets, local restrictions that fragment the working day, and deliveries that collapse when no one is home. The project addresses all three without reinventing the vehicle from scratch, working instead at the level of the cargo module, the tools inside it, and the service wrapped around it.

## The brief

Standard vans obstruct urban mobility, so the right format sits between a scooter and a light van: compact enough for restricted zones, large enough to carry a meaningful load. ZTL zones, narrow delivery windows, and environmental regulations require a vehicle that complies by default, not by workaround. And every failed delivery multiplies cost and emissions, so the system needs to decouple the delivery from the moment the recipient happens to be home.

## The cargo module

The cargo bay is the structural core of the project. Designed as a standardised unit independent from any specific chassis, it can be retrofitted onto existing vehicles through a simple attachment system, with every component positionable in any order and repeated across a mixed fleet. The module is also stackable, with a second unit able to sit on top of the first where ceiling clearance allows.

Access points were completely reorganised: two rear doors open the full width of the bay, a lateral sliding door gives access mid-route without repositioning, and a motorised floor rail moves packages toward the opening automatically, so loading and unloading no longer requires the driver to climb inside. The bay itself is built on a tubular steel frame with composite panel cladding, with the base housing the battery pack in the electric configuration. Interior shelving runs on standardised rails shared with the cobot cart, making the robot and shelf units interchangeable in position.

The bay measures 1530 mm in width, 1371 mm in height, and 2035 mm in total length, on a track width of 931 mm.

## AGADEXO

Repetitive heavy lifting is the leading cause of long-term injury in delivery work. AGADEXO is a wearable exoskeleton designed as a structural component of the vehicle rather than a stored accessory, replacing the passenger seat entirely. A single hydraulic arm activates the moment the cab door opens: the exoskeleton pivots outward and descends to standing height, the driver steps into it without bending down or reaching inside, the door closes, and the mechanism resets, all in under ten seconds.

Three quick-release buckles at the shoulder, torso and arm allow emergency removal at any point, with the safety system redundant by design so that any single buckle is sufficient to disengage the unit completely. Integration was studied against the AGADEXO shoulder unit by Agade and the ABB SWIFTI CRB 1100 cobot.

## The parcel label

Existing shipping labels are designed entirely for machine reading: dense, visually undifferentiated, and difficult to parse at a glance, working for sensors but not for people. A new label system was designed from scratch around a chromatic coding logic compliant with ISO 3864 and OSHA 1910.145: **Regular** in green (#89C273), **Fragile** in yellow (#FCC046), **Temp.** in blue (#006FB9), **Urgent** in red (#D70911), and **Hazard** in black with a yellow accent (#2D2926).

Each label is organised in three zones. The top carries the delivery type in large format, readable from across the bay. The middle carries the company logo, order code, and sender and recipient addresses. The bottom carries ISO-compliant condition icons and a barcode for automated reading. A matching adhesive tape repeats the order code across all seams in the same colour, so the parcel stays identifiable even when stacked or partially covered.

## The app

The service around Me-Moe was designed as a complete UX flow: booking, confirmation and tracking. The mental model is ride-hailing applied to parcels, so the user does not fill a shipping form, they book a vehicle. In booking, destination, parcel type, weight and dimensions go in first, the app shows available Me-Moe units filtered by proximity and time slot, and one tap confirms. In tracking, live vehicle position appears on a map with current route and estimated arrival, so instead of telling you where the parcel has been, the app tells you where it is right now. The app covers three flows, book, track and return, with login through Google, Microsoft or Meta, and a palette of hot pink, lime and teal.

<div class="split-section-text" markdown="1">

## The process

The project moved from a SWOT analysis of the proximity logistics sector through vehicle sizing, cargo bay structure, technology integration, label design and digital service design, with each layer built in response to a specific failure identified in the research phase. Modelling and rendering were done in Blender, graphic and label design in Adobe Illustrator, layout in Adobe InDesign, and FLUX.1 for contextual reference imagery.

Supervised by Consuelo Nava, with co-supervisors Carmine L. Quistelli and Domenico Lucanto.
